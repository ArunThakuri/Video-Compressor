How it works

Why does this need a server when the optimizer doesn't?

The optimizer runs entirely in this tab because patching an MP4 is just reading and writing bytes you already have. Downloading is different: YouTube, TikTok and Facebook only serve media to a real program that speaks their protocol, and browsers are blocked from doing that directly. So a small companion server runs yt-dlp and streams the finished file back to you.

The file goes from the source site, through your own server, to your phone. It is not stored afterwards — the server streams it and deletes its temporary copy.

It says the server is waking up. Is it broken?

The free hosting tier sleeps after 15 minutes with no traffic. The first request after that takes about a minute to wake it. Leave the page open — it retries on its own.

Where does the file go on my phone?

Your browser's normal download location. On iPhone that is Files → Downloads; on Android it is Downloads. From Files you can move it into Photos, or share it straight to another app. Nothing is saved inside this page, so the tab can be closed as soon as the download starts.

Which quality should I pick?

Best gives up to 1080p and is right for almost everything. 720p and 480p are smaller and faster to fetch. Audio extracts MP3 only, which is useful for music and podcasts.

On the free tier, 1080p is the practical ceiling — a longer 4K download can run out of the container's memory part-way through.

Can I download just part of a video?

Yes, for YouTube. Once the video is read, a Clip box appears: set a start and an end, like 1:30 and 1:45, or plain seconds. Only that section is fetched, so a 10-minute video you need 30 seconds of downloads in a fraction of the time.

By default the cut is a stream copy — no re-encode, so quality is untouched — but it can only start on a keyframe, which may be up to a second or two before the time you asked for. Tick Exact cut to re-encode the two edges when the cut point matters more than bit-exactness.

Other sites do not support section downloads reliably, so the box only appears for YouTube links rather than failing halfway through.

Is this legal to use?

That depends on the video and where you live. Downloading your own uploads, Creative Commons material, or content you have permission to keep is generally fine. Re-uploading someone else's video usually is not. Use it for what you have the right to download.
